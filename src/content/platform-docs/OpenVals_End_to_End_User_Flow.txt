End-to-End User Flow
Website · Customer Application · Admin Console · Backend
1. Purpose and Scope
This document explains how visitors, organization users, enterprise administrators, and OpenVals platform staff interact with the OpenVals platform. It describes user journeys, business rules, system responsibilities, and expected outcomes across the public website, Customer Application, Admin Console, FastAPI backend, PostgreSQL, background workers, and external services.

3. Phase 1 — Acquisition
Visitor explores product and pricing pages, selects Start Free Trial, and is redirected to `/auth/signup?trial=15&source=website`. The backend must validate trial eligibility; query parameters are context, not authoritative entitlement values.
Outcome: visitor reaches the Customer App signup form.
4. Phase 2 — Tenant Creation
User submits name, email, password, and organization name through `POST /auth/signup`. Backend validates input and uniqueness, creates Organization and Owner, assigns the owner role, creates a trialing subscription (15 days, 10 seats), issues access and refresh tokens, and routes the user to `/command-center`. Create these records transactionally.
Outcome: authenticated owner with a newly provisioned trial organization.
5. Phase 3 — Team and Roles
Owner invites a member and selects an organization role. Backend checks authorization and seat capacity, creates an invitation, and sends an email. Invitee opens `/accept-invite?token=...`; backend validates token, expiry, invitation status, organization status, and seat capacity before creating membership. If full, return `402 SEAT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` without partial membership.
Outcome: member joins the tenant with the selected role.
6. Phase 4 — Self-Service Billing
Owner opens Billing, selects Team monthly or annual, and starts Razorpay Checkout. Backend verifies the payment through `/organization/billing/razorpay/verify` and reconciles provider webhooks. Activate the subscription only after server-side verification. Support cancel at period end, resume, renew, seat changes, and a renewal banner when renewal is within five days.
Outcome: active subscription with plan entitlements.
7. Phase 5 — Subscription Access States
Lifecycle states include `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `suspended`, `expired`, and `canceled`. Backend computes entitlement mode: `full`, `read_only`, or `billing_only`. Past-due and unpaid states restrict writes according to policy. Backend enforcement is authoritative; frontend restrictions are supplementary.
Outcome: access reflects the current subscription and billing state.
8. Phase 6 — Enterprise Provisioning
Account team confirms the commercial contract. Platform staff creates an organization, assigns Enterprise plan, records contract period, net terms, PO number, agreed price, and payment method, then invites `enterprise_admin`. Enterprise provisioning is manual and contract-managed; no self-service trial or checkout is assumed.
Outcome: enterprise tenant and administrator are provisioned.
9. Phase 7 — Enterprise Customer App
Enterprise admin signs into the Customer App. Backend validates membership and role. Billing is read-only and displays “Managed by your account team” with the contract card. Organization roles do not grant Admin Console access; attempts to enter the console are rejected.
Outcome: enterprise workspace access without platform administration.
10. Phase 8 — Platform Login Gate
Staff opens `/login` and submits `POST /api/auth/login`. Backend authenticates and checks for at least one `platform.*` permission. Organization-only accounts receive `403`; missing or expired sessions receive `401`. Set `ov_admin_access` and `ov_admin_refresh` as appropriately secured HttpOnly cookies, load `/auth/me`, and redirect to `/overview`.
Outcome: only authorized platform users enter the Admin Console.
11. Phase 9 — Console Operations
AdminShell displays permission-filtered navigation: Platform (Overview, Organizations, Users); Access (Roles & Permissions, Plans); Billing (Subscriptions, Invoices, Deliveries); Governance (Audit log). Staff can edit profiles, suspend/reactivate organizations, edit subscriptions/contracts, extend trials 1–365 days, end trials, manage teams, issue invoices, manage role permissions, and create/edit/archive plans. Every write creates an audit entry.
Outcome: controlled and traceable platform administration.
12. Phase 10 — Invoice, Payment, Reactivation
Staff creates an invoice with line items, due days, and notes; invoice moves from draft to issued. Email sends hosted link and PDF. Customer opens the public invoice page and pays online via `/public/invoices/{id}/pay` or staff records an offline payment reference and method. After verified/reconciled payment, `mark_invoice_paid` updates invoice/payment records and reactivates an eligible subscription, resets the applicable period, removes suspension when allowed, and recalculates entitlements.
Outcome: paid invoice is reconciled and eligible customer access is restored.
13. Phase 11 — Nightly Billing Sweep
Scheduler starts the billing sweep. Worker evaluates trial reminders D-7/D-13/D-15, past-due suspension, invoice due reminders, and overdue reminders. Deduplication keys prevent duplicate messages. Email outcomes, webhook events, and billing deliveries are logged. Confirm whether trial offsets mean days remaining or elapsed days.
Outcome: recurring billing actions and notifications run reliably.
14. Phase 12 — Password Reset
Customer App uses `/auth/forgot-password`; Admin Console uses `/forgot-password`. Backend creates a 30-minute single-use token and sends an email. User opens `/reset-password`, submits a new password, and backend validates token and password, invalidates the token, and revokes all existing sessions. Responses should not disclose whether an account exists.
Outcome: password reset succeeds and previous sessions are revoked.
15. Core Business Rules
Organization roles are valid only within the Customer Application; platform permissions govern the Admin Console.
Backend authorization and tenant isolation must be enforced on every protected API.
Payment redirects do not prove payment; verify signatures and provider events server-side.
Payment, webhook, invoice, subscription, and entitlement processing must be idempotent.
Seat checks must occur before membership creation; failed checks must not leave partial records.
Administrative writes and sensitive billing changes must be auditable.
Subscription status and entitlement mode must be calculated by backend policy.
